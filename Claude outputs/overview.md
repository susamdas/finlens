## Key findings

- **Account ownership in World rose 4.9 percentage points between 2021 and 2024, to 78.7%.** _(World aggregate · 2021 73.8% → 2024 78.7%)_
- **Women’s account ownership in World remains 4.2 percentage points below men’s (76.6% vs 80.9%).** The gap narrowed by 0.7 percentage points since 2021. _(World aggregate · 2024 · women and men)_
- **62.1% of adults in Developing economies made or received a digital payment in 2024; this rose 7.3 percentage points since 2021.** _(Developing economies aggregate · 2021 54.8% → 2024 62.1%)_
- **54.5% of adults in Developing economies saved money in 2024, but only 39.7% saved in an account.** The difference reflects saving outside the formal system — at home, in savings clubs or with other people. _(Developing economies aggregate · 2024 · saved any money vs saved at a financial institution or mobile money account)_
- **About 1.3 billion adults across the 141 surveyed economies have no account.** FinLens estimate: each economy’s adult population × share without an account, summed. _(141 economies · 2024 · Findex adult population and account ownership)_
- **Among developing regions, account ownership ranges from 52.9% in Middle East & North Africa to 83.3% in East Asia & Pacific.** High-income economies, grouped separately in Findex, stand at 94.9%. _(Findex regional aggregates (excluding high income) · 2024)_
- **Kyrgyz Republic recorded the largest increase in account ownership: +27.2 pp (2021–2024).** _(Kyrgyz Republic · 45.1% → 72.3%)_
- **Libya saw the largest decline in account ownership: −32.6 pp (2017–2024).** _(Libya · 65.7% → 33.1%)_
